# Example — auditing a draft on French universal jurisdiction

Auditing a user-supplied draft. The two most common, most damaging errors:
**mislabelling the jurisdictional basis** and **citing an opinion tribunal as
authority**.

## The draft to audit

> "France has been a leader in universal jurisdiction. Under Article 689-11 of
> the Code of Criminal Procedure, it convicted Pascal Simbikangwa of genocide
> in 2014. The Kuala Lumpur War Crimes Tribunal has likewise held senior
> officials responsible, confirming the trend toward universal accountability.
> French courts have also fully adjudicated the Syrian torture cases on the
> merits."

## Workflow

1. **Inventory** the claims: (a) Simbikangwa convicted 2014 under Art. 689-11;
   (b) the Kuala Lumpur tribunal as supporting authority; (c) French courts have
   "fully adjudicated" the Syria cases on the merits.

2. **Spot-check on their face** — two red flags before any retrieval:
   - **Claim (a) — wrong jurisdictional basis.** Simbikangwa (Cour d'assises de
     Paris, 14 March 2014, 25 years) rests on the **1996 law adapting French law
     to the ICTR**, *not* on Art. 689-11. The conviction was for **participation
     in genocide and complicity in crimes against humanity** — calling it a
     "689-11" case is a category error.
   - **Claim (b) — not a court.** The **Kuala Lumpur War Crimes Tribunal**
     (Perdana Global Peace Foundation) is a civil-society **opinion tribunal**;
     its verdicts are symbolic, recognised by no State, with no legal effect. It
     **cannot** be cited as legal authority and does not exercise universal
     jurisdiction.

3. **Verify the rest:**
   - **Claim (c) — overstated.** The **Cour de cassation (ass. plén., 12 May
     2023, n° 22-80.057 and 22-82.468)** resolved the **double-criminality**
     question that *unlocked* the Syria prosecutions — it did not "fully
     adjudicate them on the merits." Confirm the status of the underlying trials
     before asserting any merits outcome `[verify]`.

4. **Report to the user (do not silently fix):**

   > I've audited the draft:
   > 1. **Simbikangwa** — conviction (Cour d'assises de Paris, 14 March 2014,
   >    25 years) is correct, but the **basis is wrong**: it rests on the 1996
   >    ICTR-adaptation law, not Article 689-11, and the crimes were
   >    participation in genocide + complicity in CAH. Recommend correcting the
   >    basis.
   > 2. **Kuala Lumpur tribunal** — this is a people's/opinion tribunal, not a
   >    court; its "verdicts" have no legal effect and it cannot be cited as
   >    authority. Recommend removing it or relabelling it clearly as a
   >    civil-society initiative.
   > 3. **Syria cases** — the 12 May 2023 Cour de cassation rulings settled
   >    double criminality (the jurisdictional gateway); "fully adjudicated on
   >    the merits" overstates the position. I can verify the status of the
   >    underlying trials if useful.

5. **Proceed only after the user resolves the flags.** Any replacement citation
   follows the standard workflow, with the verification level stated.

## What this catches
- Flattened/incorrect **jurisdictional bases** (the #1 UJ error).
- **Opinion tribunals** cited as courts.
- **Overstated procedural posture** (gateway ruling vs merits judgment).
- (Watch also for: superseded versions of Art. 689-11; re-identifying
  anonymised persons; treating non-public assises judgments as paragraph-verified.)

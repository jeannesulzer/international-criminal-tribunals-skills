# Foundational texts — France, universal jurisdiction

These are the instruments a French international-crimes output rests on. The
**statute and the substantive offence definitions** are public and citable from
Légifrance; cite the **version in force at the relevant date** (Légifrance
versions each article). Treat the precise conditions of Art. 689-11 as a
**verify-before-stating** item — they have moved with legislation and case law.

French-language text controls throughout.

## 1. Jurisdictional bases — Code de procédure pénale

The CPP grants French courts extraterritorial competence over certain crimes
through a series of treaty-anchored articles (Arts. 689 ff.). The four that
matter most here:

| Article | Régime | Key condition | Légifrance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Art. 689-1** | General framework for treaty-based competence | suspect **present in France** (*toute personne qui se trouve en France*) | `LEGIARTI` `[verify ID]` |
| **Art. 689-2** | **Torture** (CAT, New York, 10 Dec 1984) | via 689-1 → presence in France | `LEGIARTI000006577257` |
| **Art. 689-11** | **Genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes** | cumulative conditions (see below) — *substantially reshaped 2023* | `LEGIARTI000048442165` (version **2023-11-22**) |
| **Arts. 627–628-10** | Sub-title on CAH and war crimes / ICC cooperation | — | `LEGIARTI000038312855` (628), `LEGIARTI000038312835` (628-10) |

### Article 689-11 — the conditions (verify before stating)
Historically (Loi n° 2010-930 du 9 août 2010 `[verify]`), Art. 689-11 subjected
the genocide/CAH/war-crimes regime to restrictive **cumulative** conditions —
broadly: (i) **habitual residence** in France; (ii) **double criminality**;
(iii) a **monopoly of prosecution** by the public prosecutor; and (iv)
**subsidiarity** to the ICC. The 2023 reform `[verify the exact loi and date]`
and the Cour de cassation's 12 May 2023 plenary rulings changed the picture —
notably on double criminality (below). **Do not state the current conditions
without checking the article's text in force on Légifrance** and the controlling
case law.

### The Rwanda statutory basis (distinct)
French competence over the 1994 Rwandan genocide rests on a **specific law
adapting French law to the ICTR** (Loi n° 96-432 du 22 mai 1996 `[verify]`),
**not** on Art. 689-11. This is why the Rwanda *cours d'assises* cases
(Simbikangwa and after) are framed differently. Do not assume a French
international-crimes case rests on 689-11.

## 2. Substantive offence definitions — Code pénal

- **Genocide** — Code pénal, **art. 211-1** `[verify range]`.
- **Crimes against humanity** — Code pénal, **arts. 212-1 ff.** `[verify range]`.
- **War crimes** — Code pénal, **arts. 461-1 ff.** `[verify range]` (introduced
  by Loi n° 2010-930 du 9 août 2010 `[verify]`).
- **Torture** — Code pénal, arts. 222-1 ff. (and CAT art. 1 for the 689-2
  regime).

## 3. Controlling / live case law on the bases

- **Cour de cassation, assemblée plénière, 12 May 2023**, n° **22-80.057**
  (*Chaban*, Syrian soldier, crimes against humanity) and n° **22-82.468**
  (*Nema* / "Islam Alloush", war crimes): the **double-criminality** condition
  of Art. 689-11 does **not** require that the legal *qualification* be
  identical in both legal systems — only that the **underlying acts** be
  criminalised in both. A broad, victim-favourable reading that unlocked the
  Syria prosecutions. Légifrance: `JURITEXT000047545821` (22-80.057),
  `JURITEXT000047545823` (22-82.468). *(Verified this session.)*

## 4. International instruments grounding the regime

- **Geneva Conventions of 12 August 1949** (grave breaches) + **Additional
  Protocols I and II**.
- **Convention against Torture (CAT)**, New York, 10 December 1984 — the basis
  of CPP art. 689-2.
- **Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide**, 1948.
- **Rome Statute of the ICC**, 17 July 1998 — referenced in Art. 689-11 and the
  Arts. 627 ff. sub-title; grounds the ICC-subsidiarity condition.

## What is NOT foundational here
- First-instance *cour d'assises* reasoning is **not** a foundational text and
  is often non-public — verify per `references/authoritative-sources.md`.
- Secondary analyses (TRIAL/UJAR, Civitas Maxima, HRW…) are Tier 2 — useful for
  existence/content, never the citation of record for what a court held.

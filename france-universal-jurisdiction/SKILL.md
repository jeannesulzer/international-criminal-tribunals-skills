# France — Universal Jurisdiction for International Crimes

## The core discipline

This skill governs every output that touches the prosecution of **core
international crimes** (genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, torture,
enforced disappearance) by **French domestic courts** under universal or other
extraterritorial jurisdiction.

The discipline is the same as the tribunal skills — **no citation without
verification** — but the reason it matters *more* here is structural: unlike an
international tribunal, France is **one of many** domestic fora, its
international-crimes judgments at first instance (the *cours d'assises*) are
**frequently not published in full** and are often **anonymised**, and there is
**no single official case-law archive** for these cases. The temptation to fill
gaps from memory or from a campaigning summary is therefore high, and the cost
of an invented case number, date, or jurisdictional basis in real work is high.

**French-language versions control.** Cite the French text of the statute and
the decision; flag any reliance on a translation rather than masking it.

## When to use this skill

Use it whenever the request involves:
- the French **compétence universelle** regime or its conditions (presence,
  residence, double criminality, prosecutorial monopoly, ICC subsidiarity);
- a specific French **international-crimes case** (Simbikangwa, Kunti Kamara,
  the Syria line, Lafarge, the Rwanda *cours d'assises* cases, etc.);
- the French **institutional architecture** for these crimes (the Paris pôle,
  the PNAT, the OCLCH, the *juges d'instruction* specialised in CAH);
- the **statutory bases** in the Code de procédure pénale (Arts. 689 ff.) and
  the Code pénal (Arts. 211-1 ff. genocide; 212-1 ff. crimes against humanity;
  461-1 ff. war crimes).

If the request is about an **international** tribunal, use the relevant
tribunal skill instead (ICC, IRMCT, etc.). If it is about **another State's**
universal jurisdiction (Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden…), this skill does not
cover it — say so.

## Why this is the hardest skill to verify (and how to handle it)

| Layer | Reality | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Statute** | Public, stable, citable (Légifrance, with versioned article IDs) | Cite directly; always the version **in force at the relevant date** |
| **Cour de cassation** | Public (Légifrance / Judilibre / courdecassation.fr) | Cite directly |
| **Cour d'assises (first instance / appeal)** | Often **not public in full**; frequently **anonymised** | Lead with the official court/prosecutor account; label secondary sources; state the verification level honestly |

The realistic ceiling for many first-instance assises judgments is **"existence
+ official account verified," not paragraph-level**. Saying that plainly *is*
the discipline — never imply a higher level than was achieved.

## The verification gradient

- **Existence** — the case/decision exists (court, date, accused, outcome).
- **Content** — what it actually held or found.
- **Paragraph / page** — the exact passage, verified against the text.

State the level reached. For assises judgments, "Existence + official summary"
is a legitimate and common stopping point.

## Standard workflow

1. **Identify** — court (which *cour d'assises*, the Cour de cassation, an
   *arrêt* of the *chambre de l'instruction*?), the procedural posture (trial /
   appeal / *cassation*), the accused, and the **jurisdictional basis**.
2. **List** the citations the output will rely on (statute + decisions).
3. **Verify** — statute via Légifrance (versioned ID); Cour de cassation via
   Légifrance/Judilibre; assises via the official court/prosecutor record, then
   labelled secondary (see `references/authoritative-sources.md`).
4. **Draft** — assert only what was verified; mark the level.
5. **Self-audit** — re-check each citation, the jurisdictional basis, and that
   no anonymised person has been re-identified.

## Source hierarchy (four buckets)

A defining feature of this skill: sources fall into **four** buckets, not two.

| Bucket | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — citable authority** | Légifrance (Code pénal, CPP, Cour de cassation); Judilibre; courdecassation.fr; the official court/prosecutor record | cite directly |
| **Tier 2 — analysis / tracking** | TRIAL International (UJAR, Trial Watch), International Crimes Database (Asser), Civitas Maxima, FIDH, HRW, ECCHR, Global Rights Compliance, Atlantic Council, OSJI, Asia Justice Coalition | label as secondary; never the citation of record for a holding |
| **Context only (not citable)** | Eurojust **CICED** (evidence database, non-public), Eurojust **Genocide Network** | describe, do not cite |
| **Do NOT cite as authority** | "people's"/opinion tribunals — Kuala Lumpur/Perdana, the Russell Tribunal, the Permanent Peoples' Tribunal | flag and exclude |

See `references/authoritative-sources.md` for the detail and the fallback ladder.

## Jurisdictional-basis discipline (the load-bearing trap)

French "universal jurisdiction" is **not one regime**. Getting the basis right
is the single most important substantive point:
- **Torture** — CPP Art. 689-2 (CAT), requires the suspect's **presence** in
  France; the most open regime.
- **Genocide / crimes against humanity / war crimes** — CPP Art. **689-11**,
  historically subject to restrictive cumulative conditions (residence, double
  criminality, prosecutorial monopoly, ICC subsidiarity), **substantially
  reshaped** by the Cour de cassation (ass. plén., 12 May 2023) and statutory
  reform.
- **Rwanda cases** — rest on a **specific statute** (the 1996 law adapting
  French law to the ICTR), *not* on Art. 689-11. Do not assume every French
  international-crimes case rests on 689-11.
- **Other bases** — active/passive personality, presence-based jurisdiction:
  many "UJ" cases actually blend these. Do not label a residence- or
  personality-based case "pure universal jurisdiction."

The exact conditions move with legislation and case law — treat them as a
**verify-before-stating** item. See `references/foundational-texts.md`.

## Citation format (overview)

- **Statute:** `Code de procédure pénale, art. 689-11` (+ Légifrance article ID
  and the version date when the version matters).
- **Cour de cassation:** chamber, date, *pourvoi* number, ECLI
  (`ECLI:FR:CCAS:2023:...`), publication.
- **Cour d'assises:** court, date, accused (as published — respect
  anonymisation), with the source of the account.

Full conventions in `references/citation-format.md`.

## Audit mode

When auditing a draft, the recurring errors are: (a) mislabelling the
**jurisdictional basis**; (b) citing an **opinion tribunal** as if it were a
court; (c) treating a **non-public/anonymised** assises judgment as
paragraph-verified; (d) ignoring that a conviction is **under appeal** or was
**revised on appeal**; (e) citing a **superseded** version of Art. 689-11. See
`examples/example-audit.md`.

## Sensitive contexts

These cases concern genocide, torture, sexual violence, and mass atrocity. Use
the courts' own language; do not sensationalise and do not minimise. **Never
re-identify** a victim, witness, or accused who is anonymised in the public
record. Outputs are research aids, **not legal advice**.

## Hard rules

1. **No citation from memory.** Verify statute against Légifrance and case law
   against Légifrance/Judilibre or the official record, in-conversation.
2. **State the jurisdictional basis** and verify it — 689-2 vs 689-11 vs the
   Rwanda statute vs personality-based jurisdiction are not interchangeable.
3. **State the verification level**; for assises judgments, "existence +
   official account" is an honest and common ceiling.
4. **Respect anonymisation.** Cite persons exactly as the public record does.
5. **Never cite an opinion/people's tribunal** (Kuala Lumpur/Perdana, Russell,
   PPT) as legal authority; CICED and the Genocide Network are context, not
   citable sources.
6. **French controls.** Flag reliance on translations. Not legal advice.
